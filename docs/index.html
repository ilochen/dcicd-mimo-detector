K-best MIMO detector

A 3 × 3 receiver estimates three complex 16-QAM symbols from six real coordinates. Each registered layer of the detector extends the surviving symbol paths by one coordinate and ranks them by accumulated absolute residual.

Four valid channel beats load the 21 upper-triangular coefficients of R, six signed 16-bit words per beat. The final three fields are padding. The fourth accepted channel beat commits the complete matrix. An accepted observation carries six signed 16-bit coordinates of z and keeps a snapshot of that matrix through the pipeline. R has twelve fractional bits and z has ten. Shifting each z word left by two aligns the binary points before the metric calculation.

At row i, a candidate adds |zᵢ − ∑ⱼ₌ᵢ⁶ Rᵢⱼsⱼ| to its parent’s cost. Equal costs retain parent rank first and amplitude order +3, +1, −1, −3 second. A bitonic comparator network ranks the children at each layer. The winner carries all six amplitudes. Each output nibble combines the real and imaginary amplitudes of one stream, with stream 1 in the most significant nibble.

The interface accepts successive observations on successive clocks. The decision and its valid bit emerge six clocks after acceptance. A gap in the input valid signal becomes a corresponding gap at the output. These are RTL cycle counts; physical frequency and area require synthesis.

The identity-channel regression uses the transmitted vector [3, −1, 1, −3, 3, −1]. Step to layer 6 to inspect its packed decision word, 0x792. The RTL scoreboard checked 4,481 decisions across 4,870 clocks against an independent reference. Coverage includes all 4,096 noiseless identity-channel words, tied costs, numeric extremes, channel reloads, valid gaps, and reset.
